## 1｜Research question: are regulations becoming executable controls?
September's important shift is not simply the number of AI policy announcements. Three different objects suggest an observable path from a **released sector standard**, to an **updated safety-governance framework**, to a **narrow service-evaluation scheme**. But each is owned by a different institution and carries its own authority. None proves another's clauses, nationwide binding status or live enforcement.

The purpose of a later Special is to reconstruct that evidence relationship with distinct dates. Earlier W36/W38/W40 conclusions remain dated products of their own original observation, not mutable summaries.

- individual listed agent-standards numbering, final attachment texts and implementation clauses: `NOT_FULLY_REINSPECTED` here

### Bounded scientific narrative
The announcement is a **regulatory publication**; a standard's implementation date and a specific vendor's technical conformity are different questions. The CAICT-origin report relates platform, multi-agent collaboration, agentic systems, applications and data-analysis scopes, but names alone do not create a uniform architecture or automatic A2A/MCP crosswalk.

The stronger research opportunity is an exact-object standards graph: identifier, publication, implementation date, normative clause, testing body and actual SUT result. A public batch approval is only the opening of that evidence chain.

### Counterevidence / falsifier
- Ministry approval ≠ all standards currently effective; a later effective date must be honored
- A third-party republisher of CAICT text is not a second official authority; verify each named standard on a registry before clause-level argument
- `STANDARD_NUMBER_EXISTS` does not prove SDK interoperability, automatic authority revocation or delegated tool safety

## 3｜2026-09-14 — AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0
### First-party institutional fact
- National Information Security Standardization Technical Committee announcement on the CAC website, dated `2026-09-14`
- source: https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-09/14/c_1791137092283345.htm
- issuer describes a third framework iteration maintaining the structure of risk taxonomy, technical response and governance
- article links a full framework document; this Special read the **announcement**, not an exhaustive clause-by-clause PDF validation

### Why this matters
A new framework version is itself a governance-object event. It lets a researcher ask whether high-permission agents, embodied systems and model execution are being addressed as **risk classes** rather than as interchangeable chatbot safety issues. The announcement confirms the new framework and its risk-response scope; any claim about an exact clause must be tied to the actual full text.

A governance framework is not an enforceable standard or court ruling merely because a national institution issued it. Nor does its publication prove platform deployment, operator licensing, security incident prevention or independent alignment evaluation.

### Counterevidence / falsifier
- distinguish policy/framework version, legally binding obligation, standard clauses and observed enforcement
- future check: exact framework chapter, normative strength, enforcement pathway and independently observable implementation

- CNNIC publication timestamp: `2026-10-08`; official URL: https://www2.cnnic.cn/n4/2026/1008/c326-11701.html
- CNNIC search-indexed primary-source excerpt reports a `BKL-AISA/T09-2026` Agent Service Capability Evaluation Guide and certificates for 9 organizations across three enterprise agent types
- **verification limitation:** full official page retrieval timed out during this 2026-11-02 check; only the source-indexed excerpt was available. It is a `PROVISIONAL_OFFICIAL_EXCERPT`, not a personally reviewed full methodology, audit trail or certificate register

### Research interpretation
A narrow service-evaluation framework is more operationally specific than an abstract national roadmap. It may define a useful evaluation object's identity and categories. Yet certification by a named organizer under its guide is not the same as **national statutory certification**, complete security assurance or proof that every tool/action path was tested.

This also exposes a time trap: `09-29 event` is not `10-08 publication`; neither is `11-02 observatory verification`. No new 2026-09-29 Daily gets created.

### Verification and falsifier
- retrieve and inspect full official evaluation guide and methodology, tested versions, certifying entity and scope
- compare issued certificates to reproducible public test results; no equivalence presumed
- do not relabel guide-based evaluation as GB/Z / GB/T national standard conformance
